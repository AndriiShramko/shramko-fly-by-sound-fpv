# FPV simulator rooms, tracks and onboarding: what established products do and what ShramkoSonicFPV should build once

Research note 03 for ShramkoSonicFPV, an open-source browser FPV simulator (WebGPU, Web Audio, PlayCanvas, TypeScript) that blind and low-vision pilots can fly by sound alone and sighted pilots can fly with headphones. Written 2026-11-07. Evidence tags: **MEASURED** (a study, a specification, a dataset or my own benchmark gives the number), **REPORTED** (a primary source such as a manual or patch notes states it, nothing was measured), **INFERRED** (my reasoning, not a fact). Numbers in brackets, such as [3], point to the Sources list.

## Summary

**What the field does.** Liftoff, VelociDrone, DRL and Uncrashed converge on small rooms (6 to 8 race seats) on the studio's own relay, the host as a role with hand-over, "private" as unlisted plus a shared name, spectating as the overflow valve, a continuous Infinite or Perpetual mode, ghosts everywhere, and boards split by drone class or "purist" rules [1][2][3][4][5] (REPORTED). Their recurring faults are empty lobbies (average concurrency of 17 to 630 players per game), guessable private names, track edits that wipe records, and a third-party back end that fails [6][7][8][1].
**Recommendation (INFERRED, evidence in sections 1 to 7).**
1. Never depend on strangers: a persistent club room, empty seats filled with ghosts near your personal best, an infinite mode, and one asynchronous weekly challenge per club.
2. Rooms: the server is the room and the host is a role; 8 seats plus spectators; locked, private and unlisted flags; invite tokens instead of passwords; one region.
3. Tracks: one immutable TrackVersion format for hand-made and generated tracks, bound to a scene version; boards keyed by track version, drone class and assist class; validated runs only.
4. Ratings: up or down votes, Wilson lower bound (z = 1.96), a "New" shelf below 5 votes, no stars [9][10].
5. Generation: observed-free voxels near the capture path, distance-transform clearance (MEASURED 1.96 s for 8.4 M voxels), gates at clearance maxima, grid A*, centripetal Catmull-Rom, a curvature speed cap, then generate-and-test against a skill-limited bot [11][12].
6. Network: WebSocket first behind the existing proxy, one asyncio container, signed join tickets, batched 30 Hz snapshots; MEASURED 400 pilots at about 55 percent of one core on localhost; WebTransport is now Baseline and can replace the state channel later [11][13].
7. Votes and onboarding: 3 options, 20 s, no vote means the next playlist entry, only with 2 or more humans; two disclosure levels and a one-question first run [14][15].

**Patterns that repeat across products.** (1) The server is the room and the host is a role: VelociDrone says the host merely steers the session while players connect to its servers [2], and both VelociDrone and DRL added role hand-over [3][2]. Liftoff's July 2023 outage shows the cost of a relay that depends on a third-party cloud: the provider removed a service the multiplayer relied on and the studio rewrote the multiplayer framework in days [1]. (2) "Private" means unlisted plus a shared name, never a real password, in Liftoff and VelociDrone [1][2] (DRL's notes only say private rooms exist [3]); I found no invite-link feature in any manual or patch note, and DRL's global open-match notification is the closest thing. INFERRED: a guessable name is weak, an unguessable invite token gives the same flow with real privacy. (3) Spectating is the overflow valve for full rooms, crashed pilots and late joiners [1][2]. (4) Continuous racing converged: Liftoff Infinite Race (Jan 2018: unlimited laps, only lap times count) [1], Uncrashed (Apr 2025) [4], VelociDrone Perpetual Race (3 Nov 2025) [5]. (5) Skill mixing is handled by handicap, not by filters: VelociDrone's Handicap class delays each start by the difference between the pilot's lap time and the slowest pilot's, so equal efforts arrive together [2]; Liftoff players asked instead for a lobby filter on camera angle as a skill proxy (2017) [18]. (6) Votes are small: DRL rooms can vote the next random track, with "Featured" maps added to the vote [3]; Liftoff has post-race "honor votes" and a rating prompt at the results screen [1].

**What players complain about** (11 forum threads and the fix lists of the patch notes; no quantified corpus exists, see section 8). Dead waiting time and no practice inside a lobby: a 2016 thread asks for a queue so people can fly solo while waiting, and in 2017 a player notes that a private one-person lobby gives unlimited laps but records no times; the developer answered with Infinite Race [18]. Empty lobbies: a DRL player in May 2018 often found no one racing; a Micro Drones player proposed a fixed weekly meeting time in 2022 (INFERRED sign of sparse lobbies) [19][7]. Skill mixing and short time-outs: DRL's default multiplayer time-out was about two minutes, too short for hard tracks when teaching friends [19]. UI friction: a "show full rooms" tick box hid servers; creating a room was slow because every installed track was validated [18]. Lag spikes each time the first racer finishes (Uncrashed 2024) [20]. Pool size: a request for per-map play counts to judge whether a ranked pool is big enough [7]. Fragility: Liftoff's "join by name" broke for names containing "-", which every default room name had [1].

**How many people are there?** Average concurrent Steam players over the last 30 days: Uncrashed 629 (peak 1,090), Liftoff 529 (868), FPV.SkyDive 235, Micro Drones 90 (185), TRYP FPV 35 (75), DRL Simulator 17 (39) (MEASURED, third-party counters, read 2026-11-07) [6]. These counts include solo play. INFERRED: with rooms of 6 to 8, a design that needs strangers to meet in a room starves in most hours, so the first release must work with one pilot and ghosts.

## 2. Tracks, timing, ghosts and ratings

**Real dimensions (MEASURED from specifications and league listings).**

| Source | Number | Note |
|---|---|---|

| MultiGP whoop | 25 in square opening (about 63.5 cm) [25] | Whoop and micro gates exist as separate shop categories [25] |

**Editors and gate types (REPORTED).** The editors described in these sources share one model (INFERRED from their feature lists): an ordered list of crossing objects (square or round gates, start/finish, checkpoints and triggers) plus avoid objects (flags, pylons, blocks). Micro Drones' 2025 builder offers gates, triggers, checkpoints, resizable blocks, spline placement, grid and point snapping and over 175 items, with a thumbnail and description per track [26]. VelociDrone has over 2,000 objects, gates sized to major leagues' standards, a "MultiGP PRO Spec" track type and a shared database: over 1,000 base tracks plus over 40,000 online [27][5]. DRL's editor (2018) has 200+ assets and gained directional gates in 2019 [3]. FPV.SkyDive's builder caps a track at 30 visible gates and 50 detection waypoints, 8 tracks per user, and erases the personal best on every edit because an edit can change the flying path [8]; INFERRED lesson: publish immutable track versions and key every time to a version.

**Showing the course (REPORTED).** VelociDrone draws an indicator in front of the next gate, edge-of-screen arrows when it is off screen, turns it red when you approach from the wrong side, in four colours [2]. Micro Drones uses an arrow for the next checkpoint and spheres for later ones, with a setting for how many checkpoints ahead to show, and in 1.0 smoothed the guide path between checkpoints [26]. Liftoff's "Checkpoint Altitude" assist makes the drone match the next checkpoint's height [28]. VelociDrone draws a "race line" from ghost data, cyan fading to magenta over the lap [2]; DRL has a "Raceline" whose visibility it improved in 2019 [3]. FAI asks that the path through an obstacle be clear and obvious to follow [21].

**Timing and fouls (REPORTED unless noted).** Real events time from the first gate (qualifying) or from the start signal (elimination); the start is a call to arm, 2 to 4 s of silence, one tone and no countdown [21]. A missed obstacle invalidates the lap unless the pilot corrects it at once and safely [21]. RotorHazard offers "hole shot" (first crossing starts lap 1), "first lap" (first crossing ends lap 1) and "staggered start" (each pilot's clock starts at their own first crossing), a minimum-lap-time filter, and win conditions first to X laps, most laps, fastest lap, fastest 3 consecutive laps [17]; MultiGP's collegiate rules ask pilots to submit their fastest three consecutive laps of the day [24]. Simulators replace judges with automatic rules: a crash resets you to the last gate passed (VelociDrone default), Event mode red-flags a crashed pilot at the next gate and ranks by lap and gate number [2], Uncrashed's double reset returns you to the last valid gate and shows delta time to the leader at checkpoints [4]. I found no published missed-gate penalty rule in any simulator beyond these.

**Ghosts and leaderboards (REPORTED).** Liftoff ghosts (2016) replay your fastest five of the last twenty runs on the same track; Micro Drones 1.0 stores a recording with every multiplayer high score [1][26]. VelociDrone Nemesis loads the ghosts of the pilots two places above and below you on the leaderboard, over 50,000 recorded flights, so racing never needs anyone online [2][27]. DRL "Opponents": Self, Top 5, or Rival 5 [3]. Boards are split to stay fair: VelociDrone only accepts times from Single Class [2]; DRL groups by drone class and keeps a stock-parts "Leaders" board [3]; Micro Drones adds a "Purist" toggle [26]. Micro Drones initially kept leaderboard scores only from multiplayer sessions [26] (INFERRED: a server-observed session is harder to forge). VelociDrone shows 15 times per page, exports CSV to seed events, and runs a weekly time trial on one track [2].

**Sharing and rating.** Steam Workshop voting is binary (up or down) and the API's score is a Bayesian average of up votes over total votes; lists sort by all-time votes, by votes in a "trend" window, or by votes up [29] (REPORTED). Liftoff added a rating prompt on the race results screen [1]; VelociDrone lets you sort tracks by total ratings [2]. YouTube reported in 2009 that the overwhelming majority of rated videos had five stars, so ratings worked mainly as a stamp of approval, and asked whether thumbs up and down would be more effective [30] (REPORTED). The mean fails for small n and the Wilson lower bound fixes it: score = (p + z²/(2n) - z·sqrt((p(1-p) + z²/(4n))/n)) / (1 + z²/n) with p = share of up votes, n = votes, z = 1.96 for 95% [9]. A coverage study found the plain Wald interval erratic and recommends Wilson (or Jeffreys) for small n [10] (MEASURED). Computed from the formula: 1/1 up gives 0.21, 5/5 gives 0.57, 20/20 gives 0.84, 45/50 gives 0.79, 90/100 gives 0.83, 450/500 gives 0.87; a Beta(3,1)-prior average gives 0.80, 0.89, 0.96, 0.89, 0.89, 0.90 for the same inputs. INFERRED choice: store up and down counts per track version, sort "Top" by Wilson lower bound, keep a separate "New" shelf for n below 5 (otherwise new tracks sink), and a "Trending" shelf by votes in the last 14 days; Evan Miller also notes that positive ratings per view or download can be more informative than per rating, so count finished runs as the denominator when available [9].

## 3. Procedural tracks inside an arbitrary scanned scene

**Prior art (REPORTED).** FPV Freerider shipped a "Racetrack Generator" in 2018 [31]. Endless FPV (released 23 Oct 2026) stitches an endless course from segments in themed "decks" of more than 20 segments each, and lets players build a custom deck [32]. I found no generator in Liftoff or VelociDrone, whose pages describe manual editors [27]. Research: Togelius et al. evolve closed b-spline tracks scored on hitting a target challenge level, progress variance and top speed, and a radial control-point layout rules out loops [33]; the AirSim Drone Racing Lab randomises gate shape, scale and pose and scores a track by curvature per unit length and next-gate visibility [34]. I found no paper on generating drone-racing courses inside a Gaussian-splat scan, so the pipeline below combines published parts (INFERRED as a whole).

1. **Classify space as occupied, free or unknown.** OctoMap models free, occupied and unmapped space: occupied from sensor end points, free from the observed ray between sensor and end point [35]. Splat-Nav turns every Gaussian into a gamma-percent confidence ellipsoid (chi-square quantile), bins ellipsoid bounding boxes into a conservative occupancy grid, dilates it by the drone radius with a MaxPool3D kernel, and lets gamma vary with local map quality [12]. It cannot see obstacles missing from the scan [12], and casually captured radiance fields show floaters and wrong geometry off the camera path [36]. INFERRED rule for "well-scanned free space": a voxel is usable only if at least K training-camera rays crossed it before their first surface hit (K of 3 to 5, tune on scenes with known errors), it lies within D of the capture trajectory (D of 1.5 to 2 m), and the opacity-weighted splat density there is below a threshold; everything else counts as occupied.
2. **Clearance field.** The exact Euclidean distance transform runs in linear time [37]. MEASURED here: SciPy took 1.96 s for 8.4 M voxels (10 cm voxels, a 25.6 x 12.8 x 25.6 m hall) and 7.0 s for 28.3 M [11]. INFERRED: generate offline in the Python API when a scene is added, store tracks as data, and never compute this in the browser.
3. **Gate sites.** Candidates are clearance maxima with value above r_min = gate half-diagonal plus margin (a 25 in whoop opening has a half-diagonal of 0.45 m; with a 0.3 m margin r_min is 0.75 m; INFERRED), the gate normal follows the free-space principal axis, and a free approach tube is required on both sides. FAI asks for free space of at least 1.5 m diameter on the optimal line and a flight path between obstacles that allows smooth flight [21].
4. **Path.** Dijkstra or A* on the 26-connected dilated grid, cost = step length + lambda / clearance squared. Splat-Nav seeds its trajectory with Dijkstra on its dilated grid before refining it [12]; Fast-Planner runs a kinodynamic path search, then B-spline optimisation with Euclidean-distance-field gradients for clearance and smoothness [38]. INFERRED: a deterministic grid search makes (scene, seed, skill) reproduce the same track, so a track can be shared as three numbers.
5. **Smooth and limit speed.** Centripetal Catmull-Rom is the only parametrisation that guarantees no cusps or self-intersections inside a segment [39]; after smoothing, re-check clearance on the distance field. Speed cap v(s) = sqrt(a_lat / curvature(s)) from a = v² x curvature (INFERRED, kinematics). The AirSim baseline planner used vmax 30 m/s and amax 15 m/s² on eight of its nine tracks (5 and 2 on the ninth), whose curvature metrics run from 0.02 to 0.09 per metre [34] (MEASURED parameters); a survey of autonomous drone racing states that speeds exceed 80 km/h and accelerations 4 g [40] (REPORTED); beginner classes should sit far below, e.g. 0.5 g (INFERRED).
6. **Difficulty by generate-and-test.** Produce 50 to 200 candidates per (scene, skill tier), fly each with a bot limited to that tier, and keep tracks whose bot lap time and crash rate fall in the target band; this mirrors the fitness terms of [33] and the metrics of [34]. INFERRED sound analogue of "next-gate visibility": the share of the path where the next gate's audio cue is unoccluded, plus turn-rate demand.
7. **Scaling (INFERRED starting values, tune by playtest).** Gate count N = clamp(round(L / s), 4, 16), L the loop length and s the mean spacing: at least 6 times the gate opening for beginners, 3 to 4 times for experts; L at most 0.6 of the longest closed free path found. Tier 1: square gates only, turns up to 60 degrees, openings 1.5 times nominal. Tier 2: add flags, hurdles, 90 degree turns. Tier 3: add dive, ladder, split-S and tower gates as defined by MultiGP [41]. FAI recommends a 60 to 120 degree turn before the first gate to reduce start collisions [21].

## 4. Multiplayer in a browser on a small budget

| Transport | Delivery | Server side | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| WebSocket | Reliable and ordered over TCP, so one lost packet stalls newer data (head-of-line blocking) [42] | Any HTTP reverse proxy | Universal; Gaffer notes the popular web games of 2017, such as agar.io, ran on it [42] |
| WebRTC data channel | Per-channel `ordered`, `maxRetransmits` and `maxPacketLifeTime` allow UDP-like delivery [43] | Signalling plus NAT traversal (INFERRED) | FPV.SkyDive's first multiplayer builds were P2P [8] |
| WebTransport | Streams plus unreliable datagrams over HTTP/3 [13] | An HTTP/3 (QUIC) endpoint [13] | MDN marks it Baseline, newly available since March 2026 [13] |

**Choice (INFERRED).** Start on WebSocket behind the existing reverse proxy and hide it behind a small transport interface, so datagrams over WebTransport can replace the state channel later without touching rooms. Reasons: at 6 to 8 pilots the packets are tiny, PlayCanvas's own multiplayer tutorials use WebSockets (Socket.IO, Colyseus) [44], and Gaffer's objection to TCP concerns packet loss (INFERRED: worst on mobile and Wi-Fi links) [45]. Drone flight needs no server physics: each browser simulates its own drone, and the server relays states and referees the race.

**Tick rate and interpolation.** Gaffer's rule (REPORTED, tuned at 2 to 5 percent loss): buffer three send intervals so two packets in a row can be lost, plus a jitter margin; he quotes 300 ms at 10 snapshots/s (350 ms used with jitter), 150 ms at 30 and 85 ms at 60 [45]. Colyseus defaults to 20 state patches per second [46]. INFERRED: 30 Hz states from each pilot, one batched snapshot per room per tick, 100 to 150 ms interpolation for remote drones (your own drone is local, so the delay only affects what you see of others). Production notes from sims: Liftoff 1.5.9 made network traffic scale with the number of active players [1]; VelociDrone staggers joins so a burst of joiners does not freeze the client [5].

**Size of a state and of a ghost.** Fiedler packs a quaternion into 29 bits (smallest three, 9 bits each) and bounds velocity to 32 m/s in 11 bits per axis [47]. INFERRED: position 3 x 20 bits, orientation 29 bits, velocity 33 bits and one flag byte give about 16 bytes per pilot state, so my 24-byte test message is conservative. A relayed room costs 24 B x 8 pilots x 30 Hz = 5.8 kB/s per listener, about 60 kbit/s down and 22 kbit/s up per pilot if each message carries roughly 60 bytes of TCP, IP and WebSocket headers (INFERRED arithmetic). Ghosts reuse the same stream: at 10 Hz plus velocity with Hermite interpolation, which Gaffer shows to be smooth at 10 snapshots/s [45], a 60 s lap is about 10 kB before compression (INFERRED). Micro Drones saves a recording with every multiplayer high score and VelociDrone holds over 50,000 ghost flights [26][27].

**Measured relay cost (MEASURED, localhost, no TLS, Windows dev box, so a lower bound).** One Python asyncio process using `websockets` and per-tick batching: 200 pilots (25 rooms of 8) used 24 percent of one core; 400 pilots (50 rooms) used 51 to 57 percent with the age of a pilot's own state in the snapshot at 36 ms median and 78 ms at the 99th percentile (this includes up to 33 ms of batching); at 800 pilots the loop fell behind (tick lateness over 2 s) [11]. The largest sim averages about 630 concurrent players in all modes [6]; if a third of them were in rooms at once that is about 210 pilots, roughly a quarter of one core here (INFERRED). Rooms are independent, so growth means sharding by room id across processes.

**Right shape for one small Docker service beside a Python API (INFERRED).** (1) A separate container with one asyncio process, in-memory rooms, WebSocket only, behind the same TLS reverse proxy. (2) The API stays the source of truth for accounts, tracks, ratings, clubs and results. (3) Join flow: the client asks the API for a room, the API returns a short-lived signed ticket (room id, pilot id, role, expiry of about 60 s), the client opens the WebSocket with it. (4) The service calls the API only for three things: validate ticket keys, persist finished runs (idempotent run ids) and load persistent rooms at start-up. (5) Colyseus gives rooms, locked, private and unlisted flags, auto-lock when full, seat reservation and reconnection out of the box [46]; adopt its vocabulary even if the code stays in Python. (6) Referee, do not simulate: accept a run only if gates were passed in order, speed stays under the class limit and positions are continuous; Liftoff patched exploits where illegal drone setups set records, which shows the risk of trusting clients [1]. Cost: a Hetzner Cloud Regular Performance plan is listed at EUR 6.49 per month excl. VAT, and EU servers include at least 20 TB of traffic [48]; 400 pilots streaming around the clock would move about 8 TB per month (INFERRED), so bandwidth is not the constraint.

## 5. Communities, persistent rooms, small populations and map votes

**How Discord and Facebook model membership (REPORTED).** Discord: roles carry permission bits (create invite, kick, ban, manage roles and more) set per server and attached to users, and a bot can edit only roles below its own highest role; an invite has `max_age` (up to 7 days, or 0 for never; default 24 h), `max_uses` (up to 1,000; 0 means unlimited, the default) and a `temporary` membership flag [49]. Facebook (since Aug 2019): "public" means anyone sees members and posts, "private" means only members do, and a separate visible or hidden switch controls whether the group can be found; old secret, closed and public map to private+hidden, private+visible and public+visible [50]. Facebook also has admin and moderator roles [51], but only the title of that help article loaded for me, so I do not cite the permission split.

**Mapping to persistent rooms (INFERRED).** A Club (a city or a community) is the membership container: roles owner, admin, member; visibility = {public, private} x {listed, hidden} as on Facebook; joining by invite link with expiry and use limit as on Discord. The Club owns Rooms (persistent, always on) and a Playlist (ordered scene and track-version entries). Room flags follow Colyseus: locked, private (reachable only by id or invite) and unlisted (hidden but still matchable) [46]. Sim precedent: VelociDrone hidden sessions joined by name or friends list, DRL private rooms with host hand-over, Liftoff private rooms by name [2][3][1].

**Keeping a small population together (REPORTED precedents).** DRL added Quickmatch (Apr 2018), a global "open match" notification (Jun 2019) and ghost bots in multiplayer (Jun 2019) [3]. Liftoff's single-player Infinite Race got bots that keep flying so it behaves like multiplayer (Jan 2022) [1]. VelociDrone has a Quick Start button, Nemesis ghosts and a weekly time trial on one shared track [2]. Uncrashed has a quick-play button and a head-to-head race against any leaderboard replay [4]. The Game Accessibility Guidelines list starting the game without navigating several menu levels, a way to practise without failure, and a preference to play online with or without people who use accessibility features that could give an advantage [52]. INFERRED recipe: (1) "Fly now" lands in your club's always-on room if anyone is there, otherwise in a room whose empty seats are filled with ghosts near your personal best (two faster, two slower, as Nemesis does); (2) a ghost leaves when a human joins, so the room never feels empty or crowded; (3) one weekly challenge per club, same track version for everyone, raced asynchronously; (4) "open match" pushes only to members who opted in.

**Rotation by majority vote (REPORTED parameters).** SourceMod's stock vote lasts 20 s (minimum 5 s), offers 5 maps (2 to 6) excluding the last 5 played, opens 3 minutes before the end of the map, can include "Don't Change", picks a map itself if nobody votes, and can run a runoff when the winner has under 50 percent; Rock-the-Vote needs 60 percent of players and waits 30 s at start and 240 s after a failed vote [14]. DRL lets a room vote on the next random track and added "Featured" maps to the vote [3]. INFERRED for an audio-first product: read the options aloud, answer with keys 1 to 4 or a controller button, 15 to 20 s, no vote means the next playlist entry (so a pilot who cannot reach the keys never blocks the room), and a single pilot gets no vote at all.

## 6. Onboarding and menus

**How the sims onboard (REPORTED).** Liftoff replaced its tutorial in Oct 2016 with a new system of 10 videos because the old one was weak and free flight taught better; it offers optional assists at several levels, including Hold Altitude and Checkpoint Altitude, and its tutorials go from assisted flight to Acro [1][28]. Micro Drones added an interactive tutorial from fundamentals to racing in Sep 2023 [26]. VelociDrone has a Quick Start button and a tutorials button that opens videos, four flight banks where Angle is the beginner-friendly one, and warns against touching PIDs unless you understand them [2]. FPV.SkyDive has a "Getting Started" module with automatic controller calibration, then a five-unit Flight School (unit 1 free, angle mode; later Acro, advanced, manoeuvres, tricks) with ranks earned as Flight Points [8]. Uncrashed added a warning in Dec 2023 that its tutorial is difficult and will be reworked [4]. DRL redesigned controller setup with a clear "Needs Calibration" message (Oct 2018) [3].

**What an expert expects in the first minute (REPORTED).** Their own rate model and profiles: VelociDrone supports BetaFlight, QuickRates, Actual, KISS and FL1 with three profiles and conversion between types [2]; Liftoff 1.4.0 supports BetaFlight, Raceflight, Actual, Quick and KISS [1]; Micro Drones added saving, loading and deleting rate profiles (May 2023) [26]; Uncrashed added KISS, RaceFlight and QuickRates in Jun 2024 and camera tilt control from an RC axis in Mar 2026 [4]. Radio: VelociDrone accepts any transmitter that appears as a USB joystick (SBUS or DSM through an adapter) and states that a controller it cannot see is an operating-system problem [2]. Camera angle doubles as a skill marker: Liftoff players asked for lobby filtering by it [18]. Browsers add a catch for us: the Gamepad API remaps only to a "standard" layout, so an RC radio arrives as a joystick with arbitrary axes and needs a learn-the-axes wizard (INFERRED from [53]).

**Progressive disclosure that does not hide (REPORTED, then INFERRED).** Nielsen Norman Group: show the few most important options first, keep the rest behind a clearly visible, clearly labelled control, and avoid more than two levels, because designs with three or more usually test poorly; it improves learnability, efficiency and error rate [15]. Accessibility guidance adds: start without multiple menu levels, interactive tutorials, practice without failure, remember all settings, allow difficulty changes during play, assist modes, screen-reader support including menus, and settings profiles [52]. INFERRED design: exactly two levels. Level 1 is four items (Fly now, Scenes, Rooms and clubs, Settings); every settings page shows at most 7 basics plus one always-announced "Expert" control that opens the same page's second level, so nothing is hidden and nothing is deeper than two steps. First run asks one question, "Have you flown FPV before?": no, then Angle mode, altitude hold, audio path guide on, and a short practice course; yes, then the expert wizard (radio axes and calibration, rate model and values, camera tilt, drone class) and a pilot profile that can be exported and imported. Assist and guide settings are recorded with every run and part of the leaderboard key, as in VelociDrone's Single Class and Micro Drones' Purist boards [2][26].

## 7. Recommendations: data model, first version, what not to copy

All of this is INFERRED from sections 1 to 6; each row names the evidence it rests on.

| Entity | Fields that must exist from day one | Why |
|---|---|---|
| SceneVersion | id, scan refs (voxel grid, splat, per-voxel confidence), units, licence, content hash; immutable | Tracks bind to a scene version, because an edit that moves the path invalidates times [8] |
| TrackVersion | id, track_id, scene_version_id, ordered `gates[]` (kind: gate, checkpoint, start_finish, flag, hurdle; pose; size; directional), start grid, laps, tier 1 to 3, length, curvature metric, par time, `generator {algo, seed, params}` or null, status; immutable once published | One format for hand-made and generated tracks; (scene, seed, tier) reproduces a track [34][21] |
| Run | id, track_version_id, pilot, room or null, mode (race, time trial, infinite), drone class, assist profile hash, lap and gate times, ghost blob, validation status | Boards are keyed by (track_version, drone class, assist class); only validated runs count [2][26][3][1] |
| TrackVote | (pilot, track) to up or down, track version at vote time; materialised up, down, finished_runs, Wilson lower bound, 14-day trend | Binary votes, Wilson ranking, "New" shelf for n below 5 [29][9][10] |
| Club, Membership, Invite | visibility public or private, listed or hidden; roles owner, admin, member; invite token (at least 128 random bits), expiry, max uses, revoked | Facebook visibility tiers, Discord invite parameters [50][49] |
| Room | id, club or null, kind (persistent, ad hoc), flags locked, private, unlisted, seats 8 plus spectators, mode (rounds or infinite), ghost_fill rule, host as a role not a process | Colyseus flags; 6 to 8 race seats in the sims I read; host hand-over exists in VelociDrone and DRL [46][1][2] |
| Playlist, Ballot | entries (track version, featured), rotation (vote, sequential, shuffle), vote policy (options 3, duration 20 s, no vote = next, minimum 2 humans, exclude last 3); per-round ballot closes at a timestamp | SourceMod and DRL vote parameters [14][3] |
| Ghost | derived from a Run: 10 Hz states with velocity, about 10 kB per minute | Reused for replays, Nemesis-style opponents, ghost-fill [47][2] |

**First version of each (INFERRED).** Tracks: hand-made and tier-1 generated tracks in the same TrackVersion table. Ratings: up or down only, Wilson lower bound at z = 1.96, "New", "Top", "Trending" shelves, a report button. Rooms: one always-on public room per club plus ad-hoc rooms reached by invite link; 8 seats; ghosts fill empty seats; no passwords; one process, one region. Playlists: sequential, with a vote after each race when two or more humans are present. Leaderboards: validated runs only, split by drone class and assist class, top-5 and near-personal-best ghosts. Onboarding: two disclosure levels and the one-question first run from section 6.

**Room lifecycle (INFERRED from the room features in section 1).** States: ghost-only, warm-up (free flight on the room's track while waiting; Liftoff added this in 2016 and dropped the wait for one-player rooms in 2018 [1]), staging (a silent 2 to 4 s gap before the start tone, as FAI specifies and RotorHazard can randomise [21][17]), racing in rounds or continuous, results with the vote, next track. Late joiners spectate until the next start; the lock flag stops joins during a race and the host can force race or spectate roles [2][3]; a crashed pilot resets to the last gate or spectates. Keep this state machine on the server, so a host leaving changes nothing.

**Deliberately not copying (INFERRED, with the evidence for each).** (1) Name-as-password private rooms that cannot be renamed [7]; use unguessable invite tokens. (2) Regional server lists and unique typed session names [2]; use ids and links, one region first. (3) Rooms that end after one race or time out after 2 minutes [19]; persistent and infinite rooms instead. (4) Five-star ratings [30]. (5) Editing a published track in place and wiping personal bests [8]. (6) A multiplayer back end owned by a third party you cannot restart [1]. (7) Boards fed by client-reported times without validation and assist keys, since Liftoff had to patch record exploits [1]. (8) Lobbies that need strangers to appear [19][6]. (9) Menus deeper than two levels and tutorials shipped with an apology [15][4]. (10) Combat and team modes (VelociDrone's combat pack raises the cap to 10) [2]; not needed to prove the product. (11) A peer-to-peer first release, which has no referee and needs NAT traversal (INFERRED) [8].

## 8. What I searched for and did not find

- A quantified corpus of player complaints about rooms. Steam's review JSON endpoint was not reachable from my environment, Reddit pages did not load, I found no public VelociDrone forum or knowledge base, and Discord server logs are not public. The complaint list in section 1 rests on 11 Steam forum threads and patch-note fix lists, so it shows patterns, not frequencies.
- What share of concurrent players sit in rooms at any time. Steam counts include solo play and no sim publishes the split.
- Room details for TRYP FPV and FPV.SkyDive (seat caps, access rules): not in their patch notes. Third-party "best simulator" guides contradict each other on seat caps (8, 20 and 30 are all claimed), so I used manuals and patch notes only.
- Published missed-gate penalty rules in any simulator beyond "reset to last gate" and red flags; a generator for Liftoff or VelociDrone; any paper that generates drone-racing courses inside a Gaussian-splat scan; a head-to-head study of Wilson versus Bayesian averages for small-n track ratings (the table in section 2 is computed, not measured).
- Facebook's admin versus moderator permission split (the help page body did not load) and Discord's support articles (blocked by a bot check); I used Discord's developer docs and Meta's 2019 newsroom post instead. Valve's Source networking wiki was also behind an anti-bot check and is not cited.
- Tiny-whoop gate standards beyond MultiGP's own 25 in listing; I did not open vendor pages, so I give no vendor range.
- Real-network and real-VPS measurements: my relay and distance-transform numbers come from one Windows machine over localhost with no TLS, so treat them as lower bounds on cost [11].
